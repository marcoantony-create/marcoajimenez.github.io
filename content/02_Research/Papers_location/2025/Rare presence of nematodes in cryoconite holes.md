## Main idea

This paper reports the rare presence of nematodes in cryoconite holes from the Lenangsbreen glacier in northern Norway, suggesting that these organisms may form a small but reproducing population in a supraglacial microecosystem where nematodes were previously considered largely absent.

## Important points

- Nematodes from the family Monhysteridae were found in cryoconite holes.
- The study combined morphological taxonomy and DNA sequencing.
- All examined specimens were female, and some contained eggs.
- Nematodes reached up to 118 individuals per gram of dry sediment.
- They were detected in 6 cryoconite holes, with 17 specimens found in total.
- Their abundance was lower than that of rotifers and tardigrades from the same holes.
- No significant relationship was found between nematode presence and measured sediment, nitrogen, or carbon parameters.

## Why interesting

- Challenges the idea that nematodes are absent from cryoconite holes.
- Adds evidence that cryoconite holes can host more complex metazoan communities than previously assumed.
- Connects glacier ecology, nematode taxonomy, and molecular identification.
- Supports the idea that supraglacial ecosystems may contain rare or overlooked animal lineages.
