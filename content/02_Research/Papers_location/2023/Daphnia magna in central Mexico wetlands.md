## Main idea  
  
This paper reports the first record of *Daphnia magna* living in a natural water body in Mexico, specifically in the protected RAMSAR wetland Ciénegas del Lerma, and suggests that its presence may be related to escape or mismanagement of laboratory strains used in ecotoxicological tests.  
  
## Important points  
  
- *Daphnia magna* was recorded in the Chimaliapan wetland, part of Ciénegas del Lerma.  
- The species identity was confirmed using morphology and COI sequences.  
- The Mexican specimens were genetically identical to laboratory strains used in ecotoxicological bioassays.  
- The detected haplotypes were related to European strains.  
- The presence of migratory waterfowl could facilitate dispersal to other water bodies.  
- The establishment of this non-native species may threaten local *Daphnia* species.  
- The study highlights the need to use native species in ecotoxicological bioassays when possible.  
  
## Why interesting  
  
- Connects ecotoxicology, biological invasions, and freshwater biodiversity.  
- Shows how laboratory model organisms can become ecological risks if they escape.  
- Highlights the value of molecular tools such as COI for tracing possible origins of introduced populations.  
- Raises conservation concerns for protected wetlands and native zooplankton communities.
