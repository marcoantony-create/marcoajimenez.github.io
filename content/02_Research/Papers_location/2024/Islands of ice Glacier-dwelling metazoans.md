## Main idea  
  
Glacier-dwelling tardigrades can form regionally distinct populations even after long periods of deglaciation, suggesting that isolated glacier habitats may act as refugia and preserve unique genetic diversity.  
  
## Study system  
  
- Organism: *Fontourion glacialis*  
- Group: Tardigrada  
- Marker: COI mitochondrial DNA  
- Samples: 263 specimens  
- Regions: Svalbard, Scandinavia, Greenland and Iceland  
  
## Important points  
  
- Genetic variation in *Fontourion glacialis* was mainly explained by geography rather than climate.  
- Populations showed limited dispersal among glaciers.  
- Local barriers can restrict connectivity even between nearby glaciers.  
- Scandinavian populations suggest possible southern glacial or low-temperature refugia.  
- These refugia may have allowed survival during extensive Holocene deglaciation.  
- The species shows complex metapopulation structure rather than one broadly mixed population.  
  
## Why interesting  
  
- Connects cryoconite ecology with phylogeography and Quaternary history.  
- Shows that glacier-dwelling metazoans can retain regional genetic structure.  
- Supports the idea that glaciers are not only transient habitats, but potential refugia.  
- Important for understanding how ice-dependent animals may respond to glacier loss.
