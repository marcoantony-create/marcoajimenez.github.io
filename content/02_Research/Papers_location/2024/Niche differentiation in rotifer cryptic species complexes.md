## Main idea  
  
This review examines whether cryptic species complexes in rotifers show ecological niche differentiation, especially in relation to environmental variables such as temperature, pH, salinity and other limnological conditions.  
  
## Important points  
  
- The review found 177 instances of genetically identified cryptic species in rotifers.  
- These records came from 101 published papers.  
- Only part of the literature contained ecological information useful for comparing niches.  
- Stronger ecological evidence was available mainly for two complexes: *Brachionus calyciflorus* s.l. and *Brachionus plicatilis* s.l.  
- In the *Brachionus calyciflorus* complex, *Brachionus fernandoi* was associated with lower temperatures than the other species.  
- In the *Brachionus plicatilis* complex, *Brachionus paranguensis* occurred in waters with higher pH.  
- The study suggests that niche differentiation can exist even among morphologically similar rotifer species.  
- The authors highlight the need for better ecological data linked to DNA-based species identification.  
  
## Why interesting  
  
- Connects cryptic diversity, ecology and species concepts in rotifers.  
- Shows that DNA taxonomy alone is not enough; ecological information is needed to understand species boundaries.  
- Supports the idea that cryptic species may differ in environmental tolerance even when morphology is conserved.  
- Useful for thinking about ecological speciation and niche segregation in microscopic animals.
